# The Megastructure
## World & Setting Document

---

## Overview

The megastructure has no name that anyone uses. It's just "the structure" or "the ship" or "here." It may have had a designation once. It may have had a purpose—a destination, a mission. If so, that knowledge exists somewhere above your clearance level, or was lost generations ago, or both.

What you know: it's vast. It's old. It's yours to maintain. And something outside is trying to kill it.

---

## Dreamlogic Architecture

The structure does not obey Euclidean space. This isn't supernatural—or if it is, no one treats it that way. It's just how things are. You learn the routes. You develop intuition.

### Principles of Dreamlogic

**Spatial Disconnect**
Systems that must connect often don't—not visibly, not logically. The fluid reservoir is outside, around two corners, past the loading bay. The machine room that needs that fluid is three decks up and half a kilometer away. There's no pipe. The fluid gets there anyway. Your job is to make sure it keeps getting there, not to understand how.

**Contextual Geography**
Spaces change meaning based on approach. The corridor from Sector 7 leads to the hub. The same corridor, approached from the hub, leads somewhere else. Veterans know which direction to face when they enter a room. Greenhorns get lost for hours.

**Emotional Proximity**
Places you need to reach urgently feel farther away. Places you're avoiding feel closer. The structure responds to something—intention, fear, focus. Or it just seems that way because you're tired and everything is too much.

**Accumulation**
The structure has been repaired for generations. Patches on patches. Organic systems spliced into mechanical ones. Mechanical systems grown over with something alive. Nothing is original anymore. Every wall has layers.

### Navigation as Skill

Moving through the structure is the core skill of the maintenance crew. Not because it requires intelligence—because it requires *familiarity*. You learn the wrong turns that are actually right. You learn which doors to ignore. You develop a sense for when a route has shifted.

New crew members are disoriented for weeks. Some never adjust. Those who do can't explain how—it's like explaining how you know which way is up.

## The Siege

### What You Know

- There is a military enemy
- They use megavehicles—structures like yours, or at least comparable in scale
- The siege has been ongoing for [uncertain duration]—long enough that it's the only reality the younger crew members have known
- The enemy's attacks cause damage that you must repair

### What You Don't Know

- Who they are
- Why they're attacking
- What they want
- What they look like
- Whether there's more than one enemy structure
- Whether the war has a front, or sides, or goals
- Whether you're winning or losing

### How You Experience the Enemy

**Damage Signatures**
Hull breaches with specific patterns. System failures that cascade in particular ways. You learn to recognize an attack's aftermath the way a doctor recognizes a disease.

**Rumbling**
Distant impacts. Vibrations through the structure's bones. Sometimes rhythmic, like breathing. Sometimes sudden, like a fist.

**Darkness**
Sectors go offline. Power diverts. You work in emergency lighting more often than not.

**Rumors**
The crew trades theories. Contradictory, evolving, unprovable.
- "It's a swarm. Hundreds of small vehicles."
- "It's one massive thing. Bigger than us."
- "They don't have crews. Fully automated."
- "They're human. They're us, from somewhere else."
- "There is no enemy. The structure is attacking itself."

No rumor is confirmed. No rumor is denied. The game doesn't know the truth either.

## Ecology & Machinery

The structure's systems are a chimera of the organic and mechanical, fused over generations of desperate repair.

### Biomes

**Grow-Decks**
Agricultural spaces lit by ancient grow-lights. The plants here aren't just food—they're air processors, thermal regulators, water filtration. When a crop fails, something mechanical has to compensate. When something mechanical fails, you coax a biological system to cover the gap.

**Algae Vats**
Vast tanks of engineered algae, green-lit from within. They produce oxygen, protein, and components for chemical synthesis. They also produce a smell that never quite leaves your clothes.

**Fungal Networks**
In the deeper sections, fungal growth has become load-bearing. It processes waste, breaks down dead material, and has started—some say—to carry signals. Maintenance here means negotiation as much as repair.

### Machinery

**The Reactors**
You don't go to the reactors. Specialized crew only. But you feel them—a hum that's always there, that you stop noticing until it changes.

**The Circulatory System**
Fluid systems that move water, nutrients, coolant, and things you can't identify through the structure's body. The pipes are visible in some sections, hidden in others, and occasionally seem to move.

**The Nervous System**
Wiring, sensors, data conduits. Mostly defunct. The structure's ability to sense itself has degraded over time. You are its senses now—its eyes in the places it can no longer see.

## Time & History

### Generational Scale

The structure has been inhabited for generations. How many is unclear—records are fragmentary, contradictory, or stored in systems no one can access anymore.

- The oldest crew members remember their grandparents' stories about their grandparents
- The destination (if there was one) has been forgotten or mythologized
- The siege has lasted long enough to be normal

### Shifts & Cycles

Time is measured in shifts. Sleep cycles are tied to work rotations, not planetary days.

- A "day" is a shift rotation
- A "week" is a maintenance cycle
- Beyond that, time blurs

### The Before

Occasionally, crew members speak of "before the siege" or "before things got bad." It's unclear if this refers to an actual historical period or a kind of collective nostalgia for a time that never existed.

## Locations (Examples)

### Sector 7-J: Atmospheric Processing

A cathedral of pipes and condensation. The air here is thick, wet, always slightly too warm. Biological filters—sheets of engineered moss—hang between coolant lines. When they fail, you replace them. When you can't replace them, you improvise.

### The Hopper Yard

An exterior space (or what passes for exterior—the "sky" is the structure's outer hull, visible through gaps in the scaffolding). Gravel piles, fluid hoppers, loading equipment. The connection to interior systems is unclear but essential.

### Crew Quarters, Block C

Small. Efficient. Personalized through years of habitation. The walls are covered with accumulated marks—notes, drawings, repairs, stains. Every bunk has a history.

### The Wound

A sector breached in an attack three cycles ago. Still not fully sealed. The crew avoids it when possible. When repairs require passing through, they move fast.

## Mood & Atmosphere

The structure is:

- **Vast** — you will never see all of it
- **Old** — everything is patched, layered, accumulated
- **Alive** — in the biological sense and in some other sense you can't name
- **Dying** — slowly, constantly, inevitably
- **Home** — because there is nowhere else

The work is:

- **Necessary** — without it, everything fails
- **Endless** — there is always more
- **Simple** — any single task is easy
- **Impossible** — as a totality, it cannot be completed
- **Meaningful** — because you choose to make it so
