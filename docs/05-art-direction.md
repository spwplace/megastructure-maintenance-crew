# Art Direction
## Visual Design Document

*Based on collaboration with spwashi*

---

## Visual Identity

The art should evoke **wondrous melancholy**—not grimdark dystopia, not cozy comfort. Something liminal between them. The structure is dying, and it's beautiful.

### Core Aesthetic: Bioluminescent Decay

The structure is ancient, layered, alive in ways both literal and metaphorical. The visual language combines:

- **Organic architecture** — Structures that feel grown as much as built
- **Accumulated repair** — Patches on patches, generations of fixes
- **Living light** — Bioluminescence, grow-light glow, warm amber against deep shadow
- **Impossible verticality** — Scaffolding, ladders, platforms stacked beyond reason

---

## Color Palette

### Primary Atmosphere

| Color | Usage | Mood |
|-------|-------|------|
| **Deep Teal** | Shadows, depths, backgrounds | Mystery, pressure, the unknown |
| **Purple/Violet** | Atmosphere, distant spaces | Dreamlike, liminal |
| **Warm Amber/Orange** | Light sources, safety | Warmth, crew, home |
| **Grow-light Green** | Bio-systems, vegetation | Life, function, hope |
| **Warning Amber** | Alerts, damage indicators | Urgency without panic |

### Color Temperature

- **Cold spaces** (deep teal, blue-purple) dominate during navigation and isolation
- **Warm spaces** (amber, orange, yellow) emerge in crew areas and near working systems
- The contrast is crucial: warmth is earned, found in small pockets

### Avoid

- Pure white (too clinical)
- Bright red (too aggressive—use amber for warnings)
- Neon/cyberpunk palettes (wrong genre)
- Desaturated gray (too lifeless)

## Environmental Design

### Architectural Principles

**Accumulation**
Nothing is original. Every surface shows layers:
- Old paint under new paint
- Pipes rerouted around obstacles that are no longer there
- Biological growth integrated into structural elements
- Graffiti and markings from generations of workers

**Organic Integration**
The line between machine and organism is blurred:
- Moss growing on coolant pipes (functionally)
- Fungal networks as load-bearing structures
- Plants wired into sensor arrays
- Flesh-like textures on mechanical surfaces

**Impossible Scale**
Spaces should feel *too large* to be inside a vehicle:
- Vistas that shouldn't exist
- Verticality that defies structural logic
- Horizons inside enclosed spaces
- The sense that you're seeing only a fragment

**Lived-In Texture**
Every space tells a story of habitation:
- Wear patterns on floors (where people walk)
- Handprints on frequently-touched surfaces
- Personal items left in work spaces
- The archaeology of daily life

### Location Archetypes

**The Depths**
- Teal and purple dominant
- Bioluminescent accents
- Dripping, humid, enclosed
- Sound: echo, drip, hum

**The Grow-Decks**
- Green and amber dominant
- Rows of vegetation under grow-lights
- Humid but warmer
- Sound: circulation fans, rustling

**The Crew Quarters**
- Warm amber pockets in cool surroundings
- Small, personal, cluttered
- Every surface used
- Sound: voices, breathing, small machines

**The Wound (Breach Zone)**
- Cold, exposed, wrong
- External "sky" visible through gaps
- Hasty repairs visible
- Sound: wind, creaking, silence

## Character Art

### Design Philosophy

Characters should feel:
- **Worn** — these people work hard, physically
- **Practical** — clothing and gear is functional
- **Individualized** — personality expressed through small details
- **Human** — not idealized, not grotesque

### Costume Elements

**Base Layer: Work Coveralls**
- Utilitarian jumpsuits in muted colors
- Variations: sleeves rolled, collar up, partially unzipped
- Patches, stains, repairs visible

**Personal Touches**
- Hair and accessories reflect individual style
- Small items (jewelry, pins, patches) hint at personality
- Posture and body language distinctive

**Tools**
- Characters shown with their tools
- Tools show wear consistent with use
- Specialized equipment varies by role

### Character Rendering

**Style Reference: Spwashi's portrait work**
- Painterly, textured
- Rich color even in muted scenes
- Expressive without exaggeration
- Detail concentrated in faces and hands

**Emotional Range**
Characters need to express:
- Exhaustion (baseline)
- Focus (during work)
- Warmth (during connection)
- Fear (during crisis)
- Dark humor (constant undercurrent)

## UI/Interface Art

### Diegetic Elements

Interface elements exist in the world:
- **Terminal screens** — chunky CRT aesthetic, scan lines, amber/green text
- **Status indicators** — physical gauges, warning lights
- **Labels and signage** — worn, multilingual, sometimes illegible

### Non-Diegetic Elements (minimal)

- Dialogue text: clean, readable font on subtle backing
- Interaction prompts: small, unobtrusive
- No floating markers, health bars, or objective indicators

### Terminal Aesthetic

Inspired by industrial control systems:
- Dark backgrounds, light text
- Monospace fonts
- Simple graphs and readouts
- Occasional glitch/corruption

---

## Animation & Motion

### Movement Philosophy

Everything has weight:
- Characters move with fatigue
- Doors open slowly, mechanically
- Systems respond with lag
- Nothing is instant

### Environmental Animation

Subtle, constant motion:
- Lights flicker slightly
- Fluids drip and flow
- Biological elements sway/pulse
- Distant machinery moves

### Character Animation

Focus areas:
- **Hands** — working, gesturing, resting
- **Posture** — changes based on context
- **Eyes** — direction, openness
- **Small fidgets** — personality in idle moments

---

## Audio-Visual Integration

### Sound and Image Together

Visuals and audio should reinforce each other:
- Dark spaces have heavier ambient sound
- Warm spaces have more organic/human sounds
- Silence is used deliberately, for tension or peace
- No music (mostly)—the structure's sounds are the soundtrack

### The Hum

A constant low-frequency presence:
- Omnipresent in most spaces
- Pitch/intensity varies by location
- Its absence is noticeable, meaningful

## Art Production Guidelines

### For Environment Art

1. Block out impossible architecture first
2. Layer accumulated detail
3. Integrate organic elements
4. Add light sources that explain color
5. Weather and wear everything

### For Character Art

1. Start with practical base costume
2. Add role-specific elements
3. Personalize with small details
4. Ensure expressiveness in face/hands
5. Consider how they'll look in different lightings

### For UI Art

1. Design as in-world objects
2. Prioritize readability
3. Add appropriate wear/damage
4. Keep consistent with terminal aesthetic
5. Less is more
