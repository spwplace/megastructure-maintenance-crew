# Megastructure Maintenance Crew
## Core Design Document

---

### Logline

A fantasy job simulation where you play as a maintenance crew keeping a vast, dreamlike megastructure running while it's besieged by an unseen military force. Navigate impossible architecture. Fix what you can. Survive alongside the only people who understand.

---

### Design Pillars

**1. Maintenance as Meaning**
The work is rote. Any single task is simple. But the structure is vast beyond comprehension, and it is *all* slowly failing, *all the time*. You are not saving the ship. You are delaying entropy. The satisfaction of completion doesn't exist here—only the brief absence of immediate crisis.

**2. Dreamlogic Architecture**
The megastructure doesn't obey spatial coherence. The machine room needs fluid from a hopper that's outside, around a corner, past a garage door, near a gravel pile—with no direct connection between them. You don't learn engineering. You learn to think like the structure thinks, which is to say: you feel your way through.

**3. Crew as Warmth**
The structure is cold and indifferent. The enemy is faceless and unknowable. The only warmth exists between people—your squad, the ones who do this work alongside you, who understand the broken logic because they've lived it. Bonds forged in crisis.

**4. Unseen Threat**
The siege is real. Military. Megavehicles. But you never see the enemy—only their effects. Hull breaches, systems failing in patterns, sectors going dark, distant rumbling. The "why" doesn't matter. You're not soldiers. You just fix.

---

### Tone

**Wondrous Melancholy.** Not grimdark, not cozy. Something between Ghibli-industrial and fever dream. The structure is beautiful in its impossibility—bioluminescent depths, organic decay, verticality that defies sense. It's dying, and you're inside it, and there's something holy about the work even when it's hopeless.

**Dream Authenticity.** The experience should feel like being inside a recurring dream. Familiar spaces that shift. Logic that works until you examine it. Threats that don't explain themselves. The feeling of urgent purpose without clear origin.

---

### Core Experience Loop

```
NAVIGATION (solo/dreamscape)
    │
    │  Point-and-click through impossible spaces
    │  Learn the structure's broken language
    │  Find what needs fixing
    │
    ▼
MAINTENANCE (task-focused)
    │
    │  Interact with failing systems
    │  Execute tangible repairs
    │  Triage under pressure
    │
    ▼
CREW (dialogue/relationship)
    │
    │  Disco Elysium-style conversation
    │  Share rumors, fears, warmth
    │  Brief rest before the next crisis
    │
    └──────────► repeat
```

---

### Two Aspects, One Game

| Dreamscape Maintenance Crew | Megastructure Maintenance Squad |
|-----------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Navigating impossible space | Executing tangible tasks |
| Alone with the architecture | Together with the crew |
| Surreal, liminal, *lost* | Grounded, urgent, *working* |
| The structure as it *is* | The structure as it *should be* |
| Exploration, discovery | Dialogue, relationship |

These modes interweave throughout play. Solo exploration sequences give way to squad coordination. The loneliness of the dreamscape makes the crew scenes warmer by contrast.

---

### What This Game Is Not

- **Not a combat game.** You don't fight. You can't affect the siege.
- **Not a puzzle game.** Navigation isn't about clever solutions—it's about learning broken spatial logic.
- **Not a survival horror game.** There's tension and threat, but not jump scares or resource scarcity mechanics.
- **Not a management sim.** You don't optimize or strategize. You respond, you cope, you endure.
- **Not a mystery with answers.** The enemy has no reveal. The structure has no explanation. Dreams don't do exposition.

### Inspirations

**Games**
- Disco Elysium (dialogue, atmosphere, impossible spaces)
- Obra Dinn (piecing together events from fragments)
- Hardspace: Shipbreaker (blue-collar space work)
- Firewatch (walking + talking, isolation + connection)

**Literature**
- Kim Stanley Robinson, *Aurora* (generation ship, closed systems, entropy)
- China Miéville (impossible architecture, the weird as texture)
- Ursula K. Le Guin (work as meaning, community under pressure)

**Film/Visual**
- Ghibli industrial sequences (Laputa, Howl's castle internals)
- Tarkovsky's *Stalker* (the Zone's dreamlogic)
- Blade Runner (scale, decay, lived-in futures)

**Other**
- @emberian's recurring dreams (the source)
- Spwashi's visual aesthetic (bioluminescent decay, impossible verticality)

---

### Success Criteria

The game succeeds if players:

1. Feel the weight of endless, necessary labor
2. Develop affection for their crew
3. Navigate spaces that feel dreamlike but internally consistent
4. Experience the siege as pressure without needing to understand it
5. Find something beautiful in the work, even when it's futile
