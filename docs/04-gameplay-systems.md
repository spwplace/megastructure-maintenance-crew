# Gameplay Systems
## Mechanics & Interaction Design

---

## Overview

The game alternates between three interconnected modes of play:

1. **Navigation** — Moving through dreamlogic spaces
2. **Maintenance** — Interacting with failing systems
3. **Crew** — Dialogue and relationship building

These modes flow into each other organically. A shift might begin with navigation (finding the problem), move into maintenance (fixing it), and end with crew (decompressing afterward). Or an attack might interrupt maintenance with urgent navigation. The rhythm varies, but the modes remain constant.

---

## Mode 1: Navigation

### Core Experience

Navigation is **point-and-click exploration** through impossible spaces. The goal isn't puzzle-solving—it's *learning*. The structure doesn't make sense, but it has patterns. You develop intuition.

### Mechanics

**Movement**
- Click to move between connected spaces
- Some connections are obvious (doors, ladders, corridors)
- Some connections are hidden or contextual (approaching from different directions yields different results)
- No running. Movement has weight.

**Spatial Memory**
- The game subtly tracks which routes you've used
- Repeated routes become "known"—your character moves through them faster
- Unknown routes trigger brief moments of disorientation

**Dreamlogic Clues**
The structure provides subtle hints about how to navigate:
- Sounds (the hum of a working system, the drip of a leak)
- Environmental details (scuff marks on floors, wear patterns)
- Crew graffiti (cryptic notes left by past workers)

### Navigation Challenges

These aren't puzzles with solutions. They're **situations with approaches**.

**Example: The Disconnect**
You need to reach the fluid hopper, but the route you know is blocked by a breach. Options:
- Find another route (longer, unfamiliar, might get lost)
- Go through the breach zone (faster, risky)
- Ask crew members if they know alternatives

There's no "right" answer. Each choice has texture, not just consequence.

**Example: The Shift**
A space you've used before has changed. The door that led to the hub now leads somewhere else. Options:
- Explore the new space (might find something, might waste time)
- Backtrack and try a different approach
- Trust your intuition and push forward

The structure shifts. You adapt.

## Mode 2: Maintenance

### Core Experience

Maintenance is **interaction with systems**—mechanical, biological, or hybrid. The tasks themselves are simple. The challenge is triage: there's always more failing than you can fix.

### Mechanics

**System Interaction**
Point-and-click interactions with machinery, bio-systems, and infrastructure:
- Read status indicators
- Identify failure points
- Execute repairs through simple actions (flip switches, connect lines, apply patches)

**Triage Decisions**
When multiple systems need attention:
- Which do you prioritize?
- What can wait?
- What will fail catastrophically if ignored?

These aren't optimization puzzles—they're character choices. Your priorities say something about you.

**Tool Use**
You have tools. They're limited:
- Basic toolkit (always available)
- Specialized tools (for specific systems, must be retrieved from supply points)
- Improvised solutions (when proper tools aren't available)

### Maintenance Types

**Mechanical**
- Seal leaks
- Replace worn components
- Reroute power/fluid flow
- Stabilize structural integrity

**Biological**
- Adjust nutrient solutions
- Transplant healthy organisms to failing systems
- Manage light/temperature cycles
- Negotiate with fungal networks (yes, really)

**Emergency**
- Hull breach sealing
- Fire suppression
- Atmosphere venting
- Evacuate-and-seal decisions

### Failure States

The game doesn't have traditional failure states. Systems can degrade, but the game continues. The consequences are:
- Environmental (a sector becomes harder to navigate)
- Narrative (crew members react to your choices)
- Atmospheric (the structure feels more or less stable)

You can't "lose." You can only do better or worse, and even that's ambiguous.

## Mode 3: Crew

### Core Experience

Crew sequences are **dialogue-driven**, inspired by Disco Elysium's approach: text-heavy, choice-rich, character-focused. This is where the warmth lives.

### Mechanics

**Dialogue Trees**
Conversations branch based on your responses:
- Multiple response options with distinct tones/approaches
- No "right" answers—all responses reveal character
- Some paths open or close based on prior conversations

**Presence Choices**
Not all interaction is verbal:
- Sit with someone in silence
- Offer help without being asked
- Be present or absent for key moments

**Relationship Texture**
Relationships aren't tracked with meters. They're tracked through:
- What characters are willing to share with you
- How they address you
- What they ask of you
- Small details in animation/portrait expression

### Conversation Contexts

**Downtime**
Between shifts. Crew quarters. Meal times. The slow conversations where people share rumors, fears, memories.

**Work**
During maintenance. Brief exchanges while hands are busy. Practical coordination with personal undercurrents.

**Crisis**
During emergencies. Clipped, urgent. What people say when there's no time for filters.

**Transit**
Moving through the structure together. Walking and talking. The rhythm of shared labor.

### Dialogue Examples

**Downtime:**
> SOLENNE: *[pushing food around their plate]* Do you think they have grow-decks? The enemy, I mean.
>
> **Response options:**
> 1. "Probably. Everything needs to eat."
> 2. "I try not to think about them."
> 3. *[Wait for them to continue]*

**Work:**
> DAURO: Okay, the readings don't make sense. The pressure here is *higher* than upstream. That's not—physics doesn't—
>
> VELL: *[without looking up]* Different physics. Structure physics. Just compensate and move on.
>
> **Response options:**
> 1. Help Dauro understand
> 2. Back up Vell's pragmatism
> 3. Admit you don't understand either

**Crisis:**
> ORRIN: Seal's not holding. We've got maybe two minutes.
>
> KETH: Everyone out. Now.
>
> **Response options:**
> 1. Follow the order
> 2. "I can reinforce it—"
> 3. Help Solenne, who's frozen

## Progression & Structure

### Shift-Based Chapters

The game is structured around **shifts**—work cycles that form natural chapters.

**A typical shift:**
1. **Briefing** — Keth outlines priorities (crew dialogue)
2. **Navigation** — Move to the work site
3. **Maintenance** — Address the problem(s)
4. **Return** — Navigate back (possibly interrupted)
5. **Downtime** — Crew interaction

### Siege Events

Periodically, attacks disrupt the normal rhythm:
- Emergency alerts interrupt current activities
- Damage patterns require rapid response
- The structure shakes, lights flicker, sectors go dark
- Crew dialogue becomes clipped, urgent

Siege events aren't cutscenes—you navigate and maintain *during* them.

### Cumulative Effects

Your choices accumulate across shifts:
- Sectors you've stabilized stay stable (mostly)
- Sectors you've neglected degrade further
- Crew relationships deepen or strain
- The structure's overall condition shifts

No score. No rating. Just a changing environment that reflects your labor.

## Interface Philosophy

### Diegetic UI

Interface elements exist within the world:
- Status readouts on in-world terminals
- Tools visible in your hands
- No floating health bars or objective markers

### Minimalism

- No minimap (you learn routes)
- No quest log (you remember or you don't)
- No dialogue markers (people don't glow when they have things to say)

### Texture Over Clarity

The interface should feel like **working equipment**:
- Slightly worn
- Functional, not elegant
- Information present but not highlighted

---

## What We're Not Doing

**No combat.** No weapons. No violence systems.

**No inventory puzzles.** Tools are contextual, not combinatorial.

**No fail states.** The game continues regardless of performance.

**No optimization.** There's no "best" way to play. Just your way.

**No answers.** The enemy isn't revealed. The destination isn't reached. The mystery isn't solved.
